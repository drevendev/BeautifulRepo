# Programs, events and contributor readiness

**Guide baseline checked: 2026-10-03.** Program-specific source rechecks are dated
below. Refreshing one program does not revalidate the others. Rules are time-sensitive:
check the organizer's current pages before advising participation. Listing a program
does not mean this repository is enrolled, accepted, eligible, sponsored or ready to
mentor contributors.

**In a private or internal repository:** these are public open-source
participation programs, not proof that a confidential repository can accept
outside contributions or count toward any event. Do not publish internal
source, invite external contributors or promise a reward based on a private
project's existence. Use the [private repository guide](private-repositories.md)
for access and disclosure limits; check each program's current rules before
claiming eligibility.

## Hacktoberfest: use the current year's model

**2026 source rechecked: 2026-10-24.** The organizer's [homepage](https://hacktoberfest.com/),
[FAQ](https://hacktoberfest.com/questions/) and
[online participation page](https://hacktoberfest.com/online) describe a month of
in-person and online activities focused on open-source AI and open-weight models.
The FAQ explicitly says **pull requests and merge requests do not count toward
Hacktoberfest rewards in 2026**. A `hacktoberfest` label or repository topic does
not make a PR count, enroll a repository or create an organizer affiliation.

**For contributors:** choose an official Fest or online activity from the current
organizer pages; follow that event's own participation and eligibility rules. DEV
Challenges have separate contest terms. Ordinary code contributions are still
welcome on their own merit, but do not promise event credit for opening or merging
PRs. Historic "four PRs for swag" instructions are obsolete for 2026.

**For maintainers:** keep a real first-success path, contribution scope and review
capacity. Accept useful changes for their quality, not to satisfy a made-up quota.
If retaining a seasonal label after the event, describe it as historical rather
than as evidence of reward eligibility; re-evaluate it after the season. Do not
manufacture low-value tasks, advertise organizer endorsement or imply that an
untested/private project is eligible.

**Scope:** this is an organizer-page review, not an event registration, submission,
award verification, organizer contact or test of any third-party contest rules.

## Google Summer of Code: prepare for mentorship, not a badge

Use the [official program](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/) and its
[year-specific timeline](https://developers.google.com/open-source/gsoc/timeline).
The detailed 2026 timeline lists February 3 as the organization application deadline
and March 31 as the contributor deadline; both precede this page's review date.
Do not advertise an open application window based on a generic summary or a prior year.
No 2027 dates are asserted here.

**Preparation proposal:** before considering an application, develop a genuinely usable
project, scoped project ideas, documented onboarding, review capacity and an honest mentor
commitment. This is our readiness advice, not a substitute for Google's selection rules.
Mentor guidance and eligibility deserve a separate source review before any application.

## Keep seasonal material honest

Record the year, source, observation date, exact claim and next recheck trigger. Recheck
before publishing event-specific instructions and whenever the program changes. During
an active season, watch for changes at least weekly; outside it, follow the next official
announcement. Do not sign up, submit applications, contact organizers or promise mentorship
on behalf of the owner without separate authorization.
