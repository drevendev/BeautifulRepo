# Programs, events and contributor readiness

**Guide first assembled: 2026-10-03.** Program-specific source rechecks are dated
below; refreshing one program does not revalidate the other. Rules are time-sensitive:
check the organizer's current pages before advising participation. Listing a program
does not mean this repository is enrolled, accepted, eligible, sponsored or ready to
mentor contributors.

**In a private or internal repository:** these are public open-source
participation programs, not proof that a confidential repository can accept
outside contributions or count toward any event. Do not publish internal
source, invite external contributors or promise a reward based on a private
project's existence. Use the [private repository guide](private-repositories.md)
for access and disclosure limits; check each program's current rules before
claiming eligibility.

## Hacktoberfest: use the current year's model

**2026 source rechecked: 2026-10-24.** The organizer's [homepage](https://hacktoberfest.com/),
[FAQ](https://hacktoberfest.com/questions/) and
[online participation page](https://hacktoberfest.com/online) describe a month of
in-person and online activities focused on open-source AI and open-weight models.
The FAQ explicitly says **pull requests and merge requests do not count toward
Hacktoberfest rewards in 2026**. A `hacktoberfest` label or repository topic does
not make a PR count, enroll a repository or create an organizer affiliation.

**For contributors:** choose an official Fest or online activity from the current
organizer pages; follow that event's own participation and eligibility rules. DEV
Challenges have separate contest terms. Ordinary code contributions are still
welcome on their own merit, but do not promise event credit for opening or merging
PRs. Historic "four PRs for swag" instructions are obsolete for 2026.

**For maintainers:** keep a real first-success path, contribution scope and review
capacity. Accept useful changes for their quality, not to satisfy a made-up quota.
If retaining a seasonal label after the event, describe it as historical rather
than as evidence of reward eligibility; re-evaluate it after the season. Do not
manufacture low-value tasks, advertise organizer endorsement or imply that an
untested/private project is eligible.

**Scope:** this is an organizer-page review, not an event registration, submission,
award verification, organizer contact or test of any third-party contest rules.

## Google Summer of Code: prepare for mentorship, not a badge

**2026 source rechecked: 2026-11-12.** Use the
[official program](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/) and its
[detailed 2026 timeline](https://developers.google.com/open-source/gsoc/timeline).
The organization application deadline was **February 3 at 18:00 UTC** and the
contributor application deadline was **March 31 at 18:00 UTC**. The last extended
contributor final-work/evaluation deadline was **November 2 at 18:00 UTC**.
The final scheduled **mentor** evaluation deadline for extended projects was
**November 9 at 18:00 UTC**. All four deadlines are past as of this review;
the deadline passing does not establish whether any particular evaluation
was submitted. November 9 was **not** a new contributor application window.

The page's automatic summary quotes different dates: use its explicit
**2026 Timeline**, not the summary. It also gives a general month-by-month
outline for future programs, but **no confirmed 2027 deadlines**; do not
reuse 2026's exact dates for applications in another year.

For mentoring organizations, Google's
[January 19, 2026 announcement](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2026/01/mentor-org-applications-for-google-summer-of-code-2026-open-through-feb-3.html)
listed an established project of at least **18 months**, an OSI-approved
license, an active community prepared to mentor, and a credible project-ideas
list among its requirements. An MIT license or a public GitHub repository alone
cannot establish BeautifulRepo's eligibility, selection or mentorship capacity.

**Preparation proposal:** before considering an application, develop a genuinely usable
project, scoped project ideas, documented onboarding, review capacity and an honest mentor
commitment. This is our readiness advice, not a substitute for Google's selection rules.
Mentor guidance and eligibility deserve a separate source review before any application.

## Prepare a project idea before applying to GSoC

**Practical reusable artifact:** copy the
[GSoC project-idea card](../templates/GSOC_PROJECT_IDEA.template.md) as a
draft. It asks for a real reader problem, a bounded deliverable, observable
tests, prerequisites, a verified first-contribution route, mentor capacity
and an explicit AI-tool policy. One completed card is **not** an application
or a complete organization ideas list.

Google's [ideas-list guide](https://google.github.io/gsocguides/mentor/defining-a-project-ideas-list)
recommends concise needs rather than a prescribed implementation, independent
projects rather than tasks contingent on another contributor, and multiple
substantive ideas for organizations considering participation. Its
[ideas-page checklist](https://google.github.io/gsocguides/mentor/making-your-ideas-page)
calls for a description, prerequisites, difficulty, prospective mentors and a
size category. The published guide currently uses approximately **90, 175 or
350 hours**, but verify those categories again for the intended program year.
A mentor name is not a commitment: Google also emphasizes
[real mentor availability](https://google.github.io/gsocguides/mentor/selecting-students-and-mentors)
and [collaborative expectations](https://google.github.io/gsocguides/mentor/setting-expectations).

**Worked scoping example (hypothetical, not offered for applications):**
for an offline README checker, "reduce false positives in an existing
Markdown-link diagnostic" is a *problem*, not a project plan. A useful core
outcome would be a set of public synthetic fixtures that reproduce named
false positives, a bounded correction to the diagnostic, and before/after
CLI results. A separate optional stretch might improve the explanation of
the finding. This does **not** claim such defects were reproduced or that
BeautifulRepo has an available mentor; no project or eligibility is being
advertised.

**AI-tool expectations:** the [2026 GSoC program lead's guidance](https://groups.google.com/g/google-summer-of-code-discuss/c/UzgaSAKgcyg)
encouraged organizations to state their own AI-tooling expectations on the
ideas page. The card makes that decision explicit rather than assuming a
universal permission or ban. This is guidance for writing an honest
public project idea, not permission to apply, recruit, run an agent or
submit work on a maintainer's behalf.

**Sources reviewed 2026-11-14.** Recheck the actual program year and current
official timeline before publishing dates or project sizes.

## Keep seasonal material honest

Record the year, source, observation date, exact claim and next recheck trigger. Recheck
before publishing event-specific instructions and whenever the program changes. During
an active season, watch for changes at least weekly; outside it, follow the next official
announcement. Do not sign up, submit applications, contact organizers or promise mentorship
on behalf of the owner without separate authorization.
